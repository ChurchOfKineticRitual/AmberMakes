# The Substitute

A short dating-sim mystery set in a West Country secondary school. **Design document v1.1**

> Story, cast, structure, and writing scope live in this document. Visual design, stack, and build plan live in the companion `the-substitute-visual-spec.md`. Where the two disagree on stack or visuals, the visual spec is authoritative — it's been written more recently with a clearer visual reference in hand.

---

## 1. The pitch

You are a supply teacher arriving at St Aldhelm's, a small secondary school in the village of Aldhelm's Cross in the West Country, for a two-week placement covering Year 10 English. The previous teacher, Ms. Eleanor Harlow, vanished without notice three weeks ago. The school is officially calling it "personal leave."

Over ten weekdays, you teach by morning and meet five colleagues at lunchtimes and after school. You can romance any of them. Each of them is hiding something — most of them small, human secrets, but together they hold the shape of what really happened to Ms. Harlow. By Friday of the second week, the truth has to be assembled or it's too late.

The truth is older than the school. The Harlow family has been cursed for nearly four hundred years, and the thing that comes for one of them every autumn is currently teaching maths down the corridor.

Tone: warm staff-room comedy underneath, slow folk-horror dread on top. Think _Derry Girls_ meets _Penda's Fen_. Funny lunchtimes. A village that has been hiding something for centuries. A maths teacher no one quite remembers.

## 2. The story

### What actually happened

In approximately 1665, **Edward Harlow**, a Somerset farmer, stumbled on a coven of seven local women meeting in a wood outside his village. Frightened — this was the height of the English witch-panic — he killed one of them, a woman named **Anne Penhale**, with a wood-axe and fled.

The surviving six did not report the murder. They cast a single working, the only one of its kind they ever made, and bound it to Edward Harlow's blood: _one of your line will die each year, taken by a thing in the shape of someone helpful, until your line ends._

The six witches died in their time. Their descendants live in the village still. Most have forgotten. Some remember and are quietly proud. One — **Iris Penhale**, Anne's direct descendant, mid-fifties, runs a small wool shop on the high street of Aldhelm's Cross — has carried the guilt of it for her whole life. She knows the shape of the working because her grandmother told her, and her grandmother's grandmother told her grandmother. She has never had the chance to undo it. No Harlow has ever come close enough.

The Harlow family is now small. Eleanor's father drowned at 41. Her uncle vanished. Her aunt was found in a wood at 38. Eleanor grew up knowing one of them dies a year and she counted the calendar. She came to St Aldhelm's last September because she thought a school full of people might be safer than a quiet life. She was wrong: she came back toward Aldhelm's Cross, the village where the curse was made.

The thing the witches conjured does not have a fixed shape. It takes whatever form lets it get close. This year, with Eleanor at a school, it wears a maths teacher. It joined the staff at the start of the autumn term, the same week she did. Its name on the timetable is **Mr. Calder**. It has been quietly invisible since.

The curse does not work as a hunter killing prey. It works as a _thing settling in_. Three weeks before the game begins, Mr. Calder reached Eleanor in the school car park after a Friday staff meeting. She has not been seen since. She is not dead. She is _fading_ — held in some half-place, less herself each day. By the Friday of the second placement week, she will be gone in a way that cannot be undone.

The player has ten weekdays to assemble enough of the story to find Iris, convince her to break with her family, and undo the working before Friday afternoon.

### What the player needs to learn

Reaching the true ending requires the player to gather:

1. **The family pattern** — that Harlows have been dying one per year for generations. (Surfaces through Ronny.)
2. **That Eleanor was scared of someone specific at the school.** (Surfaces through Nathan and Michael.)
3. **Which staff member she was avoiding.** (Surfaces through Penny's notebook and Nathan's research.)
4. **That there is a village layer to this.** (Surfaces through Nathan's memory of Eleanor mentioning a shop, or Penny's notebook records.)
5. **How to approach Iris.** (Surfaces through Loala's recollection of Eleanor saying _"I shouldn't get close to anyone right now."_)

Each colleague's romance route surfaces a different piece. No single route gives you all five. The player needs at least three threads' worth of understanding to break the curse.

### The three endings

- **Bad ending — Friday, end of day.** You haven't pieced it together. Eleanor is gone. The school carries on. Mr. Calder leaves at the end of term.
- **Pyrrhic ending — Friday afternoon.** You worked out what happened. You confronted the beast. Eleanor is still gone, but you stopped _this_ shape of it. The curse will continue. The colleague you trusted most stands beside you and the scene plays out with them.
- **True ending — Friday afternoon.** You found Iris in time. She broke with her family. The working was undone. Eleanor returns, changed. The Harlow family is free for the first time in nearly four hundred years. The colleague you trusted most is the one who walks her out of the wood.

## 3. The cast

### Ms. Eleanor Harlow

- **Age:** 28
- **Role:** Year 10 English teacher; first year at St Aldhelm's
- **What she was like:** Quiet, watchful, very good with the awkward kids. Read constantly. Knew her family history and lived with the count.
- **Voice (when remembered):** Dry humour, careful, never quite let anyone in. Said things like _"I shouldn't get close to anyone right now"_ and was misread.

The player never meets her except in artefacts: her marking, her chair, a coat still on a peg, a book Penny stole, a voicemail Nathan kept.

### Mr. Michael Jamey — Deputy Head

- **Age:** 38
- **Notices:** Some notices have been taken from the staff-room pin-board overnight.
- **Voice:** Usually quite calm; as soon as Eleanor goes missing he starts talking very frantically.
- **Wants from you:** Keep the students calm. Don't let it spread.
- **Hides:** They hadn't actually broken up. They broke up the night before she disappeared — _she_ broke it off, telling him she didn't want him pulled into something coming for her. He thought she was being cryptic and ended it angrily. He hasn't told anyone they were still together because he's terrified of being the obvious suspect. The frantic energy is guilt mixed with grief.
- **Relationship to Eleanor:** Was her boyfriend until the night before she vanished.
- **What his route surfaces:** That Eleanor was scared of something specific in the last fortnight. That she chose to push him away to protect him. (Combined with another route, this becomes "she was scared of someone _at the school_.")

### Coach Loala FeldLinn — PE Teacher

- **Age:** 26
- **Notices:** Some balls have gone missing from the PE cupboard. (Red herring. It's the Year 8s.)
- **Voice:** Bossy with everybody, but went quiet around Eleanor. Now bossy as armour.
- **Wants from you:** To prove you are worth her time.
- **Hides:** A week before Eleanor vanished, Loala finally told her how she felt. Eleanor said no, kindly, and said _"I shouldn't get close to anyone right now."_ Loala took it as rejection and stopped speaking to her. They never made it up. Loala thinks Eleanor disappeared believing Loala resented her.
- **Relationship to Eleanor:** Best friend; always wished they could have been more.
- **What her route surfaces:** The phrase _"I shouldn't get close to anyone right now."_ Crucial later for approaching Iris — it tells the player that Eleanor knew this was coming and was actively isolating herself.

### Mr. Nathan Davis — Science Teacher

- **Age:** 42
- **Notices:** Muddy fingerprints on the space display.
- **Voice:** Warm and loving even under pressure. Avuncular. Devastated.
- **Wants from you:** Companionship. He doesn't expect it to become romance; if you push it there, he is moved and a little frightened.
- **Hides:** Eleanor told him she was scared of someone at the school. She wouldn't say who. He has been coming into the building at night for the last two weeks, going through staff records, trying to work out who. The muddy fingerprints are his. He's checked every colleague's records — except the maths teacher's, because he genuinely keeps forgetting Mr. Calder exists.
- **Relationship to Eleanor:** Childhood friend; known her since the first day of secondary school. Met her family. Knows their history.
- **What his route surfaces:** Everything. The family pattern; that she was scared of someone specific; eventually, when prompted, that there's a shop in the village she mentioned more than once. He is the most important route — and also the most expensive in time, because he is wary and slow to open up.

### Ronny Flax — Caretaker

- **Age:** 62
- **Notices:** All the sinks in the girls' bathroom had been turned on overnight. _(This is real. When the beast feeds, water in the building behaves oddly. It happened the night Eleanor vanished. Ronny noticed and said nothing.)_
- **Voice:** Bossy, rude, demanding.
- **Wants from you:** Go away. Stop asking questions.
- **Hides:** Nothing personal. He just disliked her — clash of temperaments, mostly. But his mother grew up in the village and he knows the old stories about the Harlow luck. He has been waiting for something to happen on his grounds since Eleanor was hired. He doesn't want to be the one who has to explain it to anyone.
- **Relationship to Eleanor:** Disliked her for no particular reason. Resented being assigned to the same school as someone he expected to lose.
- **What his route surfaces:** The family pattern — that this has happened before, generations of it, that locals know the name _Harlow_ the way locals know any old name. Cracking Ronny open is hard but the information is the foundation of everything.

### Mrs. Penny Harrington — Literacy Teacher

- **Age:** 29
- **Notices:** Two books missing from the bookshelf. _(She took them. They were Eleanor's.)_
- **Voice:** Rambling, often ends a conversation on a completely different topic. Warm but scattered.
- **Wants from you:** A new partner, after she broke up with her girlfriend last spring. Slightly too soon. Slightly rebounding.
- **Hides:** She had a slightly obsessive crush on Eleanor. She kept a notebook tracking what she'd learned — Eleanor's routines, what she read, where she went after school. Two books missing from the staff bookshelf are Eleanor's; Penny took them. She is mortified by what she did. But the notebook is also the most detailed record anyone has of Eleanor's last weeks — including which colleague Eleanor was actively avoiding, and that Eleanor visited a small wool shop in the village three times in her last fortnight.
- **Relationship to Eleanor:** Quietly besotted. Watched from a distance. Never said a word.
- **What her route surfaces:** The data. The notebook contains the staff member's name (a footnote: _"avoids Mr. C in the corridor"_) and the shop's name and street. Cracking Penny open emotionally — convincing her the notebook isn't shameful, it's evidence — is the route's beat.

### Mr. Calder — Maths Teacher

- **Age:** "Mid-thirties." Always.
- **Role:** Joined the staff at the start of the autumn term. Year 9 and 11 maths.
- **Notices:** Nothing. Has noticed nothing all term. Is happy to help. Polite. Tidy.
- **Voice:** Pleasant. Quiet. Doesn't tell stories. Doesn't have a first name anyone uses. _"Just Calder is fine."_
- **What he is:** The shape the curse wears this year. Has been since September. Will leave at the end of the autumn term either way.
- **How he's written:** He should appear in the early game a handful of times, in passing — a hello in the corridor, a polite offer to share a kettle, a wave from a doorway — and the player should not notice him. He is described in the prose in flat, forgettable phrases. He never has dialogue longer than a sentence. He is never given a section heading. When the player finally realises, the moment should land _because_ he was there the whole time.

### Iris Penhale — the guilty witch

- **Age:** 54
- **Role:** Runs a small wool shop on the high street of Aldhelm's Cross, the village St Aldhelm's serves. Lives above it.
- **Appearance:** Careful, watchful. Cardigans. A long grey plait.
- **Voice:** Quiet. Says less than she knows. Watches the door.
- **Wants:** To finally do the right thing. Has wanted it her whole life.
- **What she knows:** The whole shape of the curse and how to break it. Has known since she was nine, when her grandmother told her at her grandfather's funeral. Has watched newspapers for Harlow deaths her whole adult life.
- **What she fears:** Her own family. Not all the coven descendants feel guilty. A few are quietly proud. If she breaks with them, there will be a cost — she may have to leave the village.
- **What she does in the game:** She appears twice in passing — once in the first week if the player wanders into the village on a free period, once more on the Saturday between weeks. The player will not notice her either time. By Wednesday or Thursday of week two, one or both romance threads (Nathan's memory, Penny's notebook) point the player back to the shop. If the player arrives with enough understanding, she opens the door. If not, she closes it.

## 4. Structure

### The two-week timetable

The game runs for ten weekdays. Each day has the same three slots:

|Slot|What happens|
|---|---|
|**Morning**|A short lesson-planning scene. The player picks one of three approaches to the day's lesson, which lightly nudges one of the three stats (Rapport, Curiosity, Discretion).|
|**Lunchtime**|A medium-length scene with one colleague of the player's choice, _if that colleague is available that day_. About 200–400 words of writing.|
|**After school**|A longer scene with one colleague of the player's choice, _if available_. About 500–900 words. This is where romance and mystery progress.|

Colleagues are not all available every day. Each has a pattern (their "routine," in the Doukyusei sense):

- **Michael** (deputy head): Available most days, but increasingly distracted as week two progresses. Frantic by Thursday week two.
- **Loala** (PE): Available at lunchtimes (after-school sports clubs Mon/Wed/Fri until 4:30pm).
- **Nathan** (science): Slow to open up. After-school only, and only on days the player has taught a "Curiosity" lesson plan (he respects that). Most expensive route.
- **Ronny** (caretaker): Only catches him in passing on first-week days. From Tuesday of week two he starts deliberately appearing where the player is, wanting them gone.
- **Penny** (literacy): Available almost every lunchtime, never after school (her own routine). Romance only progresses if you've also helped her with her Year 9 marking, which she'll ask you about on day three or four.

This is a _Doukyusei_-style schedule grid. The player can't see all the colleagues every day. Choices have a real cost.

### Mandatory beats (story spine)

These fire on specific days regardless of what the player does:

- **Day 1 (Mon week 1)**: Arrival. Brief introduction to all five colleagues plus a glimpsed Mr. Calder. Lunchtime is fixed: Michael shows you the staff room.
- **Day 3 (Wed week 1)**: Ronny finds you in a corridor and tells you to ask fewer questions. First small wrongness — you notice Mr. Calder in the staff room and immediately forget him.
- **Day 5 (Fri week 1)**: First-week wrap. Whichever colleague you've spent the most time with has a small evening moment — a pub visit, a walk, a shared cigarette outside.
- **Day 6 (Mon week 2)**: A book of Eleanor's marking turns up in a drawer that was empty on Friday. (Forces engagement with the mystery layer.)
- **Day 8 (Wed week 2)**: A _significant_ wrongness — depending on routes, this is when Penny shows you the notebook, or Nathan tells you what he knows, or Loala finally cracks.
- **Day 9 (Thu week 2)**: The player must declare an intention — go to the village, confront Mr. Calder, or report it. Branching from here.
- **Day 10 (Fri week 2)**: The ending. Plays out based on what you've gathered and who you're with.

### The three stats

|Stat|What it represents|What it gates|
|---|---|---|
|**Rapport**|How well the kids respond to you.|Whether Michael trusts you enough to confide. Whether Penny relaxes enough to show the notebook.|
|**Curiosity**|The kind of teacher you're being.|Whether Nathan opens up. Whether you spot small wrongnesses in scenes.|
|**Discretion**|How quietly you're moving.|Whether Ronny tells you anything. Whether Iris opens the door.|

Stats are nudged by morning lesson choices (three options per day, each tilts one stat by +1) and by some after-school scene outcomes. They never go above ~8 over the two weeks, which is fine — most gates are at threshold 3 or 5.

The point is **not** that the player optimises. The point is that the kind of _teacher_ you choose to be lightly shapes who opens up to you. A Discretion-heavy player gets Ronny and Iris. A Curiosity-heavy player gets Nathan and the mystery. A Rapport-heavy player gets the warmth — Michael, Penny, Loala — and less of the spine.

## 5. Scene inventory

A rough count for scoping. Numbers are _scenes_, not screens or paragraphs.

|Category|Count|Notes|
|---|---|---|
|Morning lesson scenes|10 × 3 = 30|Short. Mostly reusable templates with day-specific content.|
|Lunchtime scenes (5 colleagues × ~5 visits each)|~25|200–400 words each.|
|After-school scenes (5 colleagues × ~4 visits each)|~20|500–900 words each. The heart of the game.|
|Village scenes (Iris shop, churchyard, Saturday wander)|6||
|Mandatory story beats|8|The spine.|
|Endings (3 outcomes × 5 colleague flavours)|15|Each ending has a "with this colleague beside you" variant.|
|**Total writing units**|**~104 scenes**|Substantial but bounded.|

This is substantial but bounded. Doable.

## 6. Technical stack

### Recommendation: **Phaser 4**

Three reasons:

1. **Phaser 4's built-in Filters system** (Pixelate, ColorMatrix, Displacement, Glow, Blur, Bokeh) gives us the exact primitives needed for the warm/cold palette logic and the Mr. Calder distortion mechanic without writing custom shaders.
2. **Phaser's Scene system** maps cleanly onto the structure of the game — each weekday is a scene, each location (staff room, classroom, village) is a sub-scene, and the four custom UI surfaces (timetable, evidence board, village map, staff room) are overlay scenes. This is the structural model Phaser is built for.
3. **Pixel-art populated isometric scenes are Phaser's home territory.** The visual reference (Sea of Stars / Eastward / Octopath register) is a world the player stands in, not a sprite-on-backdrop visual novel layout. Visual-novel engines fight this; Phaser is shaped for it.

The trade-off vs. a visual novel engine like Ren'Py: Ren'Py gives you save/load, dialogue advance, and branching script syntax for free, at the cost of fighting it for any non-VN layout. Phaser gives you total visual control and a populated-world model, at the cost of building the dialogue-advance and save/load machinery (small, bounded — a few components).

The companion visual spec covers the stack reasoning in more depth, including the per-feature mapping (palette, distortion, scenes, fonts).

### Art and audio

For the first build, **placeholder art is fine**. Phaser runs perfectly on coloured rectangles labelled "MICHAEL" while you write the scenes. Once the script is working, swap in proper sprites and backgrounds. Audio can come last or not at all.

The visual style is pixel art in the Sea of Stars / Eastward / Octopath Traveler register — dense, painterly, with volumetric lighting. Full specifications (resolution, sprite cell sizes, palette, typography) live in the visual spec.

## 7. Staged build plan

Don't try to build the whole thing at once. Ship something playable at each stage.

### Stage 1 — Skeleton (target: one weekend)

- Phaser 4 project running
- Day 1 plays through, with intro scenes for all five colleagues
- A lunchtime choice that picks a colleague and plays a placeholder scene
- A day-end screen that advances to day 2
- Save/load working

**Output:** A playable five-minute slice that demonstrates the loop.

### Stage 2 — The week-one loop (target: 1–2 weeks of work)

- All five days of week 1 implemented
- Each colleague has lunchtime and after-school scenes for week 1
- Stats tracked and visible
- Routine logic working (some colleagues aren't available some days)

**Output:** A playable first week. ~15 minutes of game. You can show it to people.

### Stage 3 — The mystery layer (target: 1–2 weeks)

- Week 2 implemented
- Mandatory story beats firing on the right days
- The village scenes added
- Iris meetable

**Output:** The full game is playable end-to-end with one of the three endings working.

### Stage 4 — Endings and polish (target: 1 week)

- All three endings written
- All five colleague-flavour ending variants written
- Stat gates tuned
- A pass on the writing

**Output:** Shippable.

### Stage 5 — Art and presentation (open-ended)

- Replace placeholder rectangles with real portraits and backgrounds
- Title screen, end credits, custom UI styling
- Optional: ambient sound
